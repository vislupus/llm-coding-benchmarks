StressLab

A 2-D structural sandbox. Build pin-jointed structures out of members, anchor them to the ground, load them, and watch how forces flow — and where things give way.

Building

  • Member tool (B): drag from a joint (or empty space) to another joint (or empty space). New joints are created automatically.
  • Anchor (A): click a joint to pin it to the world; click empty space to create an anchored joint.
  • Load (L): click a joint to hang the chosen weight on it. Click again to remove.
  • Select (V): click to select, drag to move, drag on empty space to box-select. Change material, cross-section, lane and weights in the Build tab. Delete/Backspace removes the selection.
  • Wheel zooms, middle-drag (or the Pan tool / H) pans, F fits the structure in view.

Simulating

  • Space runs and pauses. Loads ramp in over the first 1.2 s so the structure settles instead of being shocked.
  • Members are coloured by force: red = tension, blue = compression; brighter means closer to the limit. Members above 75 % utilisation pulse as weak points. Ductile materials yield (yellow centre line) and stretch permanently before rupturing; slender members buckle under compression well below their crushing strength; cables go slack rather than pushing.
  • Reset returns to the design you had when you pressed Run. You can keep editing while the simulation runs to test repairs on the fly.
  • Stress test ramps the applied loads until the first member fails and reports the load factor it reached.

Notes on the model

Joints are frictionless pins, so stability comes from triangulation — a rectangle without a diagonal will fold. Units are metres, kilograms and kilonewtons; stiffness and strength are tuned so deflections are visible at human scale, so treat the numbers as relative rather than code-grade engineering values.
