A guide to the workbench

Build. Apply. Observe.

01 / Build a system

Start with a preset or a blank canvas. Add joints, then click pairs to connect members. Struts resist stretching and compression; cables only pull. A frame tool adds diagonal bracing. Meshes are flexible spring networks. Anchor joints to fix supports. Orange load points share the added weight.

02 / Test it in motion

Run or step the model and adjust conditions live. Drag a free joint during a run to tug it. Paused dragging edits the starting design and resets the test. Ground movement shifts the supports horizontally. Circular bodies collide with members, each other, and the floor.

03 / Read the response

Color shows axial force divided by the member's modeled capacity. Red means over capacity; sustained overload can fracture members. Peak utilization and maximum drift retain the highest readings in this test. Drift excludes moving bodies. Select a member to inspect its force, strain, and damage.

04 / Explore conditions

Pressure acts normal to marked exposed members, using a 1 m modeled depth. Wind is a simplified horizontal drag force with optional gusts. Rain is normalized wetness: it adds retained water mass and weakens materials over time. It is not a rainfall-rate or fluid-flow model.

What this model can tell you

This is an illustrative 2D particle-and-member simulation with compliant distance constraints. It is useful for comparing behaviors within the app. It does not calculate beam bending, torsion, full rigid-body rotation, fluid flow, or realistic buckling. Material values are deliberately simplified, not engineering design allowables. Use the results to explore ideas, not to certify real structures.
