Field notes

A model for discovery.

This is a conceptual animal cell, built from procedural 3D geometry. Every animation is generated in this file; there are no libraries, images, network requests, or asset downloads.

Explore the cell

Drag to orbit. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Select a label or a structure name for an explanation, then use “Focus in 3D.” Checkboxes hide individual structures; legend buttons filter particle types. The timeline follows one illustrative secretory pathway. The mRNA marker hands off to a new protein marker at translation: RNA does not become protein.

What the model simplifies

  • Sizes, distances, colors, molecule counts, and relative stage durations are chosen for clarity. Glows are visual markers, not emitted light. Motion follows prescribed paths plus small oscillations; this is not a molecular dynamics or quantitative kinetics simulation.
  • Only a subset of newly made proteins enters the ER. Free ribosomes also make cytosolic proteins. ER/Golgi transport is more complex than the single forward route shown, including retrieval, recycling, and cargo sorting.
  • Six duplicated chromosomes represent an illustrative genome, not a human karyotype. The model starts after DNA replication in interphase. Each daughter ultimately receives six chromosomes. Chromosome shapes and spindle attachments are schematic.
  • Interphase precedes mitosis. The “Prophase” section includes prometaphase and nuclear-envelope breakdown. Cytokinesis overlaps late mitosis in real animal cells; it is separated on the teaching timeline.
  • The actomyosin ring pinches the membrane during cytokinesis; microtubules separate chromosomes. Organelle redistribution and the rebuilding of ER/Golgi are simplified. Transport markers are hidden during mitosis to keep the division machinery readable; cellular metabolism does not simply stop.
  • Mitochondrial ATP markers represent energy-carrying molecules exported to the cytosol. ATP is also made by glycolysis, which is not drawn. Lysosomal enzyme markers remain inside lysosomes.

Controls & access

Space play / pause · ← → ↑ ↓ orbit · + / − zoom · R reset view · L labels · M switch mode. All controls are keyboard accessible. Reduced-motion preferences start the simulation paused. Switching away from this tab pauses its clock.

Rendering notes

Native WebGPU uses instanced meshes, a depth shadow map, weighted blended transparency, GPU-computed molecular paths, and a glow / tone-mapping pass. Transparency and soft shadows are approximations, not optical microscopy. WebGPU support depends on the browser, GPU, drivers, and secure context; see the WebGPU specification.
