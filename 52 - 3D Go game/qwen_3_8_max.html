Two colours, one grid. Fence off territory, strangle enemy strings, and out-read an opponent built on Monte Carlo tree search — rendered on lacquered kaya wood.

How a match unfolds

Black moves first; stones are placed on intersections and never move. A string with no adjacent empty points is captured. You may not recreate the immediate previous position (ko) or play a suicidal stone. Two consecutive passes end the game: each side scores its surrounded empty points plus prisoners; White receives 6.5 komi. If the computer marks stones dead that aren't, keep playing instead of passing.

Reading the board

Play

Click any intersection to place a stone. Translucent preview = legal, red ring = forbidden (occupied, ko, or suicide). The vermilion ring marks the last move.

Camera

Drag to orbit · wheel or pinch to zoom · double-tap / C resets the view.

Controls

P pass · U undo (takes back your move and the AI's reply) · pass twice to score.

Scoring

Japanese style: surrounded empty points + prisoners; White adds 6.5 komi. All stones on the board count as alive — resolve dead stones by playing them out before passing.

The AI

Every AI turn runs Monte Carlo tree search in your browser: it grows a game tree, plays out thousands of random-but-informed futures, and backs wins up the tree. Difficulty sets simulation depth, exploration and tactical sharpness.
